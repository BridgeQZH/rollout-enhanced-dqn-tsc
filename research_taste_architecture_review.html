Verdict in one paragraph. This project studies one clean mechanism — a one/multi-step lookahead over an analytic store-and-forward transition model f and stage cost g, bootstrapped by a frozen DQN tail value H — and asks when that lookahead recovers performance the frozen network leaves on the table. The two empirical answers (it compensates under-training, and it re-opens a large gap off-distribution at heavy demand, with a variance/tail-truncation mechanism) are supported by committed CSVs that I re-verified: the repository’s own recompute-from-source harness passes 41/41 checks against the shipped data. The reward/tail-value alignment (reward ≡ g so β = 1), SMDP-correct γΔ discounting, route-hash-asserted paired design, and demand-shedding guard show genuine research care rare at this project scale. The main weaknesses are scientific scope rather than execution: the algorithm is a textbook truncated rollout (Bertsekas-style) applied to one symmetric SUMO junction; the “n pairs” statistics pool model seeds and traffic seeds as if independent; the converged model has only two seeds; the fixed-time baseline is untuned; the state omits the current phase (a formal MDP gap); and the headline lookahead depth (3) comes from a hard-coded fallback rather than the config. Overall: a serious, credible research prototype with a publishable kernel that needs broader scenarios and stronger baselines to become a paper.

Scope caveat of this review Four directories referenced throughout the README are gitignored and absent from this checkout: models/ (all DQN checkpoints), deck/ (slide deck + EVIDENCE.md), docs/ (narrative reports + reference paper PDF), and academic_artifacts/ (statistical summaries consumed by scripts/extract_evidence.py). Also missing despite README references: activate.ps1/activate.sh and SUMO_GUI_RECORDING_GUIDE.md. Claims that depend on those artifacts (e.g. training-curve convergence of the 500-episode model) could not be independently verified.

3 · Reconstructed Research Story

In plain English, the project’s scientific narrative is:

  1. Problem. Control a single 4-way signalized intersection to minimize total vehicle delay (the integral of queue length over time). Deep RL controllers work here, but they are expensive to train and brittle when real traffic drifts away from the training distribution.
  2. Hypothesis. Both failure modes — under-training and distribution shift — are the same thing: residual approximation error in the learned value function. A short online lookahead (“rollout”) grounded in cheap traffic physics can correct that error at decision time, spending test-time compute instead of training samples.
  3. Mechanism. Score each candidate phase u by g(x,u) + β·γΔ·H(f(x,u)): an exact analytic cost for the next signal window plus the frozen DQN’s estimate of everything after. The physics model f only needs to rank actions correctly, so it stays valid off-distribution where H degrades.
  4. Findings. (a) With a 100-episode under-trained DQN, depth-3 rollout recovers ≈85% of the gap to a 500-episode DQN (paired p = 0.019). (b) On the converged model the advantage washes out at nominal demand (p = 0.55) — reported as a confirmation, not hidden. (c) Stressing demand 1000 → 2000 vehicles/episode re-opens the gap monotonically to a ≈15% delay reduction (p ≈ 1.7×10⁻¹⁰), explained by tail truncation: rollout clips the DQN’s catastrophic worst-case episodes (max delay 99,586 → 43,389 veh·s; CV 0.259 → 0.099).
SUMO microsimulationintersection/environment.net.xml — ground-truth dynamics, 4×750 m arms, 8-phase light. Vehicles: Weibull(2) departure profile, 25% turners (env.generate_routefile).
Observation x ∈ ℝ¹²env.get_state() — per-lane-group vehicle counts within 100 m of the stop line (STOPLINE_ZONE_M, constants.py:176).

↓
Rollout controller (evaluation-time only) agent.select_rollout_1s / select_rollout_ms — argmax over candidate first phases of accumulated analytic g’s (greedy-g base policy beyond step 1) plus discounted DQN tail at the leaf. Compared against dqn / greedy / fixed_time under identical, hash-verified traffic (eval.py).

The connection between the pieces is the project’s strongest conceptual asset: g is simultaneously the training reward, the rollout stage cost, and (approximately) the evaluation objective, so the lookahead score and the learned value live on one scale and the rollout weight β = 1 needs no tuning. That alignment is enforced in code, not just prose (agent.py docstring “sign convention (locked)”; train.py:162).

4 · Algorithmic Interpretation

4.1 What is actually implemented

Five controllers share one DQNAgent (agent.py):

ModeDecision rule (as implemented)Needs
fixed_timeDeterministic cycle 0→1→2→3 (choose_action_fixed_time), i.e. a 56 s cycle given green = 10 s, yellow = 4 s.nothing
greedyargmaxu g(x,u,uprev) (greedy_base) — myopic, serves the currently most-costly unserved lanes. Structurally a queue-based max-pressure-like rule without downstream terms.g
dqnargmaxa Q(x,a) with ε = 0 at evaluation (choose_action).Q
rollout_1sargmaxu [g(x,u) + β·γΔ·H(f(x,u,λ))] (select_rollout_1s).g, f, λ, Q
rollout_msDepth-h lookahead: first action enumerated, steps 2…h follow the greedy-g base policy on imagined states, cumulative γΣΔ discounting, DQN tail at the leaf (select_rollout_ms). Arrival rates λ held constant across the imagined horizon.g, f, λ, Q

4.2 Where rollout sits in the pipeline

Rollout is used only for evaluation-time action selection. Training (train.py) is plain ε-greedy DQN: rollout never shapes exploration, replay targets, or policy improvement during learning. This matches the stated “frozen prior + test-time search” framing exactly — the implementation is faithful to the idea. It is a one-shot rollout in Bertsekas’s sense (single policy-improvement step over base policy + terminal cost approximation), not an iterated rollout/AlphaZero-style training loop.

4.3 Correctness details that were done right

  • SMDP-consistent discounting. Actions are temporally extended (10 s green, +4 s yellow on switch). Both the replay bootstrap (Sample.discount = γΔ, agent.replay) and the rollout tail use the same per-transition γΔ — a subtle point most TSC-DQN codebases get wrong by using a per-decision γ.
  • Reward/tail commensurability. reward ≡ g (train.py:162) makes β = 1 principled rather than a tuned hyperparameter.
  • No lexicographic tie-break bugs. Scores accumulate into float arrays with np.argmax (called out explicitly in docstrings — evidence of a past bug hunt).
  • f-fidelity is measured, not assumed. Every rollout evaluation logs the predicted-vs-realized next state (log_residual); the honest answer (Spearman ρ ≈ 0.54, MAE ≈ 0.24 veh) is committed in analysis_summary.json.

4.4 Gaps and quiet discrepancies

Headline hyperparameter comes from a hard-coded fallback All configs ship lookahead_depth: 1, and Settings even forbids rollout_ms with depth < 2. The evaluation harness silently substitutes DEFAULT_MULTISTEP_DEPTH = 3 (eval.py:71,363-367) — confirmed by eval_manifest.json (“multistep_depth”: 3). The depth that produces every headline number is therefore invisible in the config system. Works, but is a reproducibility trap.
  • The state omits the current phase. The true state of this SMDP includes u_prev (it determines whether a yellow is inserted and the switch cost), and the rollout score conditions on it — but the Q-network input is only the 12-D queue vector. H(f(x,u)) cannot distinguish “arrived at x′ mid-phase-0” from “mid-phase-2”. Formally the DQN solves a slightly aliased POMDP. Likely a small effect with switch_penalty = 0, but it is exactly the kind of gap a reviewer will find.
  • Vanilla DQN without a target network. agent.replay bootstraps from the online network; training is Vidali-style episodic offline (800 gradient steps after each episode). No Double-DQN, no gradient clipping, no terminal masking (the last transition of each episode still bootstraps). Acceptable for the inherited chassis, but the “converged 500-episode DQN” claim rests on a learner with known instability modes and no committed training curves to inspect.
  • Training reward is the analytic surrogate, not the realized cost. R = g(x,u,u_prev) is computed before execution from the current state (assumes unserved queues constant over the window, ignores arrivals within it), while evaluation uses realized queue-seconds from SUMO. The two are strongly correlated but not identical; the DQN optimizes a model of the objective, not the objective.
  • One-step rollout does not actually work at 100 episodes. The committed phase-3 report (clean_rollout_tlcs/eval_results/phase3_paired_benchmark_report.html) shows rollout_1s losing to bare DQN at 100 ep (−0.75% wait, 47.8% win rate); only depth-3 rollout_ms wins (+1.12%). The README’s method section is written around the one-step formula, but the headline evidence comes from the multi-step variant. This variant-selection nuance should be surfaced, not left in a sub-report.
  • Constant-λ imagined futures. select_rollout_ms reuses the current arrival-rate estimate for all imagined steps — fine at depth 3 (~30–42 s), but an undocumented approximation of the lookahead.

6.2 Where the evidence is weaker than it looks

  • Pseudo-replication in the paired tests. “n = 90 pairs” is 3 model seeds × the same 30 traffic seeds; “n = 60” is 2 × 30. Pairs sharing a traffic seed (and pairs sharing a model) are correlated, so the Wilcoxon and JT tests overstate the effective sample size. The p = 0.019 sample-efficiency result is the fragile one — at D = 2000 the effect (rank-biserial 0.95) survives any correction, but the 100-ep claim needs a hierarchical/per-model-seed analysis or more independent traffic seeds.
  • Two seeds for the converged model. evidence.json → checkpoints confirms dqn_500ep has seeds {0, 1} vs {0, 1, 2} for 100 ep. Every stress conclusion rests on two training runs; a third seed is cheap insurance against a lucky/unlucky converged prior.
  • “Converged” is asserted, not shown. Training curves are written per run (train._save_series) but live under gitignored models/. Nothing committed demonstrates the 500-ep model plateaued, or how variable training was across seeds.
  • Variant selection. The treatment in every headline analysis is rollout_ms (depth 3, itself a hard-coded fallback — §4.4). rollout_1s, the variant the method section is written around, is worse than DQN at 100 ep and only ties at 500 ep (per-mode means in evidence.json). No depth ablation (1/2/3/5) is committed, so “why depth 3?” has no evidence-based answer.
  • Baselines are internal only. fixed_time (untuned), greedy-g, and the DQN itself. No Webster-timed fixed plan, no SUMO actuated control, no max-pressure, no published RL method. For the paper-level claim (“rollout is a robustness mechanism”) the DQN-vs-rollout pairing is fair and well-controlled; for any “this is a good controller” claim the baseline set is inadequate.
  • Train/eval demand separation is partial. Traffic seeds are provably disjoint (eval 100000–100029 vs train s·10⁴+e with s ≤ 2 — verified in training_settings.yaml:51-54 and train.py:53), which is real leak hygiene. But the nominal evaluations use the same demand level and profile shape as training, and the stress evaluation varies only volume. Generalization across demand shapes (peaks, asymmetry) is untested.
  • Schema drift between experiment generations. The nominal CSVs lack throughput/residual_vehicles; the stress CSVs have them. The anti-shedding guard therefore protects only the stress claims, and stale comments in stress_sweep.py:74-90 still describe the pre-extension schema.

6.3 Visualization

7 · Code Architecture Review

7.1 Strengths

  • Clear module boundaries around the research objects. f lives in a SUMO-free module (transition.py), sensing and g in the environment, control paths in the agent, the network in model.py. Dependency injection (cost_fn, transition passed into DQNAgent) keeps the agent simulator-agnostic.
  • Config discipline. One Pydantic Settings model with cross-field validators drives every entry point; run directories get a YAML snapshot and a JSON manifest (train.py:205-229, eval._write_manifest). Constants are centralized and explained (constants.py is exemplary).
  • Reproducibility mechanics. Global seeding (set_global_seeds), deterministic disjoint route-seed schedules, MD5 route-hash assertion inside the benchmark loop (eval.py:398-400), pinned requirements, versioned state_dict-only checkpoints with weights_only=True loading.
  • Self-contained statistics (no SciPy) that are then cross-checked by an independent recompute harness — a defensive pattern that paid off (41/41).
  • Docstring quality is unusually high; sign conventions and locked design decisions are documented at the point of use.

8 · Research Taste Evaluation

What shows genuine taste. The best decision in this project is what it chose not to claim. The washout at convergence (p = 0.553) is promoted to a headline finding — “rollout is a compensation mechanism, not a free asymptotic gain” — instead of being buried. The demand-shedding guard, throughput-normalization invariance check, route-hash pairing assertion, causal (lagged) arrival sensing, and the recompute-from-source validation harness are all answers to attacks a skeptical reviewer would make, built before the reviewer asked. The f-fidelity number (ρ ≈ 0.54) is unflattering and committed anyway, with the correct defense (f need only rank actions). The unifying frame — amortization vs test-time compute, with under-training and distribution shift as two faces of one value-approximation error — is a real idea, articulated crisply, and the tail-truncation mechanism (CV 0.259 → 0.099, worst case halved) is the kind of mechanistic explanation most empirical RL papers never attempt.

Where the taste falls short. First, algorithmic novelty is thin: truncated rollout with a learned terminal cost is textbook approximate dynamic programming, and “test-time search fixes value-network error, most when the network is weak or off-distribution” is the established lesson of the model-based search literature. The contribution is an application-domain instantiation with good measurement — legitimate, but it must be framed that way. Second, the f/g formalism is partly decoration: g genuinely earns its keep (it is the reward, the rollout stage cost, and the objective at once), but f is a four-line store-and-forward update whose “Phase 2 calibration” is asserted without committed evidence, and the one-step controller built most directly on the f/g story is the variant that doesn’t work at 100 episodes. The intellectually honest version of the story is “depth-3 lookahead under a greedy base policy”, and the repo knows this (all headline treatments are rollout_ms) while the prose still leads with the one-step formula. Third, the experimental scope was chosen for cleanliness over stress: one symmetric junction, one demand shape, volume-only distribution shift, internal baselines only. That makes the result solid but small — the +1.12% nominal effect would not survive review as a sample-efficiency claim without the correlation-structure fix, and the impressive +15% result lives in exactly the regime (spillback past the 100 m sensing zone) where the state representation is weakest and least analyzed. Fourth, the “PhD-interview deck” orientation of the tooling suggests the project was optimized to present a finding at least as much as to pressure-test it; the missing depth/β ablations and the two-seed converged model are the symptoms.

Overall: this reads as the work of someone with strong experimental hygiene and above-average scientific honesty, one good mechanism-level insight, and not yet the breadth of evidence (or literature positioning) to convert it into a publishable contribution. Research taste 7/10: high on rigor and honesty, middling on novelty and scope ambition.

Level 2 — medium-effort scientific strengthening (weeks)

  1. Fix the statistics’ correlation structure. Analyze per model seed and aggregate with a mixed-effects model (or report per-seed Wilcoxon + a meta-combined p), instead of pooling 3×30 correlated pairs into “n = 90”. Train a third (and fourth) 500-ep seed so both regimes have ≥3 independent priors.
  2. Depth and β ablations. Sweep depth ∈ {1, 2, 3, 5} and β ∈ {0, 0.5, 1} across demand levels; β = 0 isolates “pure model-predictive greedy” from “DQN tail matters” — currently the single most important missing ablation, because it tests whether the learned component contributes anything at D = 2000 or whether f/g lookahead alone explains the robustness win.
  3. Stronger baselines. Add Webster-timed fixed control, SUMO’s built-in actuated logic, and max-pressure (trivially implementable on the existing 12-D state and SERVED_LANES). The greedy-g controller is close to max-pressure already — make the comparison explicit and cite it.
  4. Demand-shape generalization. Evaluate under asymmetric arms (e.g. 70/30 N-S/E-W), two-peak profiles, and turn-ratio shifts — all reachable by parameterizing generate_routefile. Volume-only shift is the easiest kind; shape shift is where the “physics shield” claim gets a real test.
  5. Add u_prev to the Q-network input (13-D or 12+4 one-hot) and re-train; either the results are robust (good — say so) or the aliasing mattered (better — a finding).
  6. Commit an f-calibration study: predicted-vs-realized scatter, per-lane bias, and the effect of saturation-flow error on the action ranking — turning the ρ ≈ 0.54 footnote into a “how good must f be?” sensitivity result.
  7. Unify the two CSV schemas and re-run the nominal benchmarks so throughput / residual guards cover the 100-ep and 500-ep claims too.

Level 3 — major research upgrade (months)

  1. Pick one thesis-grade question and scale it. The strongest candidate is the robustness story: “online physics-grounded lookahead as a certified safety net for learned signal controllers under distribution shift.” That framing supports: multiple junction geometries, a demand-shift taxonomy (volume / asymmetry / composition / incidents), and uncertainty-aware rollout budgeting (invoke search only when the DQN’s value ensemble disagrees — directly monetizing the 9.5× latency cost the repo already measured).
  2. Multi-intersection extension (already on the README roadmap): an arterial of 2–4 signals with a per-junction rollout and shared f. This forces the refactor in §7.2 (network description object instead of module constants) and is where the method could differentiate from single-junction max-pressure.
  3. Learned-residual f. Fit a small correction model on the already-logged residuals (the hooks exist: agent.log_residual) to push rank correlation past the ≈0.54 ceiling, with an ablation isolating its contribution.
  4. Rollout in the training loop (approximate policy iteration): use rollout as the behavior policy or target-improvement operator and compare against frozen-prior rollout — connecting the project to the modern search-amortization literature it implicitly invokes.
  5. Position against published TSC-RL baselines (max-pressure variants, PressLight/MPLight-class methods) on a standard scenario set, or explicitly scope the paper as a controlled mechanism study and defend that choice in the introduction.

10 · Main Risks

CategoryRisk
Scientific (1) The D = 2000 headline may be partly an artifact of the 100 m observation zone: under saturation the DQN’s state is truncated exactly when decisions matter most, so the “value-error” being corrected may be substantially observation error — the β = 0 ablation would disambiguate and is currently missing. (2) The +1.12% sample-efficiency effect may not survive correlation-corrected statistics. (3) Volume-only shift may overstate f’s “off-distribution validity”; a shape-shifted demand could break the constant-λ lookahead assumption.
Engineering No tests around the analytic core or the self-rolled statistics; duplicated dispatch and the copy-pasted _simulate override are drift-prone; single-intersection constants block the roadmap’s own MARL extension.
Reproducibility Checkpoints, deck, docs, and intermediate summaries are gitignored — a fresh clone can verify committed CSVs (strong) but cannot regenerate them without retraining (weak). The headline depth lives in a code fallback, not config. sweep_plan.json records paths (model_long/…) that don’t match the documented layout, so the exact provenance of the stress checkpoints is not fully traceable from the repo.
Interpretation Readers will over-read three things unless warned: the −43% vs fixed-time number (untuned baseline), “rollout” meaning the one-step f/g formula (headline evidence is depth-3 with a greedy base), and “robustness” meaning general distribution shift (only demand volume was shifted). Latency (9.5×/decision) is honestly measured but the real-time feasibility argument (4 ms ≪ 10 s decision period) is left implicit.

11 · Final Verdict

Classification C — serious research prototype, at the upper end of that band. It is clearly more than an engineering demo: there is a stated hypothesis, a controlled paired design, pre-registered-style guards against the obvious confounds, honest negative results, and a self-auditing analysis pipeline that reproduces from committed data (41/41). It is not yet near-paper-ready (D): the mechanism is textbook, the scope is one symmetric junction with one demand shape, the statistics pseudo-replicate, the converged condition has two seeds, and the decisive β = 0 / depth ablations that would pin down why rollout wins under saturation are absent.

To reach D (near-paper-ready): fix the correlation structure of the tests and add a third+ converged seed (§9 L2.1); run the β and depth ablations to separate the physics lookahead’s contribution from the learned tail’s (§9 L2.2); add max-pressure and actuated baselines (§9 L2.3); demonstrate at least one non-volume demand shift (§9 L2.4); commit checkpoints and training curves so every claim is regenerable; and reposition the contribution honestly as a controlled mechanism study of test-time search in signal control, citing the rollout / ADP lineage explicitly. With those six items this becomes a defensible workshop-to-conference submission; the writing, statistics infrastructure, and demo assets are already ahead of that bar.
